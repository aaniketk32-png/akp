00 · Why I made this

Ayurveda has a story. Most skincare ads don't.

The problem

Skincare ads all sound alike: "glowing skin", "clean ingredients", "clinically proven". Shoppers scroll past because they have heard it all before.

Ayurveda has something most brands don't: centuries-old rituals, real ingredients like saffron and turmeric, and a slower, kinder idea of beauty. My job would be to make that story stop the scroll and earn the click.

Who I'm writing for

Linda, 52, Ohio

Dry, tired-looking skin. A shelf full of serums that didn't deliver. She notices her skin on video calls.

Her doubts: "Won't a face oil feel greasy?" and "Is Ayurveda real, or just a trend?"
What she wants: fewer products, a visible glow, and a routine that feels like self-care, not homework.

The idea: Less routine, more ritual. Three drops, one quiet minute, every night. Everything below is built on it.

01 · Hook conceptualization

Five hooks for the first 3 seconds

Each one opens a video ad. They are built to make Linda stop scrolling before she decides to.

  • "Indian grandmothers have done this for centuries. I'm only just catching up." Story

03 · Long-form sales copy

The sales page: opening and structure

The opening sets up the problem and the promise. The structure walks a doubtful reader all the way to checkout.

Headline and opening

Why a 1-minute Ayurvedic ritual might replace half your serums

Meet Kesaradi Daily Glow Oil: saffron, turmeric, rose and Ayurvedic herbs in one light oil.

Count the bottles on your bathroom shelf. Now count the ones that actually made a difference. If the answer is "maybe one," you're not alone.

Modern skincare keeps adding steps. Ayurveda goes the other way. For centuries, Indian households have turned to saffron and turmeric for simple skin rituals. Not ten products. One oil, warm hands and one quiet minute before bed.

Kesaradi Daily Glow Oil is built on that idea. A few drops at night, pressed gently into clean skin. In the morning, skin looks softer, smoother and brighter. And your routine finally fits into your life.

Page structure

  1. The problem: too many products, too little difference
  2. The old answer: the Ayurvedic ritual, in plain words
  3. What's inside: saffron, turmeric, rose, and what each one is known for
  4. How to use it: three steps, one minute
  5. Proof: real customer reviews and photos
  6. The guarantee: 30 days, or your money back
  7. The offer: pack of two saves more
  8. FAQs: greasy? sensitive skin? how long does a bottle last?
  9. Final call to action

06 · Product naming

Three names for the new mask

The names fit iYURA's style (Kesaradi, Balaayah, Yauvari): rooted in Sanskrit and Hindi, but easy to say in the US and UK.

08 · How I'd know it's working

Copy is only good if it converts

Ads

Hook rate (who watches past 3 seconds), click-through rate and cost per click. Test three hooks per ad and keep the winner.

Sales page

Scroll depth and conversion rate. If people stop at the ingredients, the story above isn't doing its job.

Email

Open rate for the subject line, click rate for the body, and orders from early access.

A note on claims: I kept every claim cosmetic ("looks brighter", "feels softer") and used only facts from your public product pages: ingredients, 3,700+ reviews and the 30-day guarantee. Final copy would go through your review team.

If one line here made you think "we could test that", let's talk.

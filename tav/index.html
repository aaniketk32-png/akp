Sample 2 · 20-second video script · Manjish Glow Elixir

0–3sOn screen: lamp clicks off, a bottle glows on the nightstand.
VO: "The last thing I do every night takes 30 seconds."
3–12sOn screen: two drops, warm palms, slow press across cheeks.
VO: "Manjish Glow Elixir. Manjishtha and lemon, the Ayurvedic way to wind down."
12–18sOn screen: morning light, fresh face, coffee.
VO: "Skin that looks rested, even when I'm not."
18–20sText: "Your night shift starts at 10. Try it for 30 days."

Why it works: sells the bedtime moment, so the product becomes a habit, and the 30-day guarantee removes the risk.

Sample 5 · Long-form sales copy · Product page

The skin around your eyes shows tiredness first. Start there.

Screens, late nights, early alarms. The skin around your eyes is the thinnest on your face, so it gives you away first. Rufolia was made for that one small area.

What's inside, in one line each

  • Manjistha: an Ayurvedic root for a brighter, more even look
  • Niacinamide: helps reduce the look of dullness
  • Hyaluronic acid: hydration for a smoother, plumper look
  • Sacred lotus and aloe: calm and moisturise

How to use

Three pea-sized dots: under the eye, at the crow's feet, on the lid. Pat in a C-shape. Morning and night.

The promise

Use it for 30 days. If you're not happy, send it back, even opened, for a full refund.

Why it works: each ingredient gets one plain line, so a doubtful reader understands the formula in ten seconds.

Sample 6 · Naming, taglines and an audio sting

A simpler name for ads

"Periorbital Eyemulsion" is accurate, but hard to say. In ads I'd call it "The 3-Dot Eye Cream". It describes exactly how you use it, and people remember a ritual faster than a word.

Other options

Rufolia Wake-Up Cream · Rufolia Eye Reset

Taglines

  • Ayurveda, with a lab coat on.
  • Ancient roots. Modern results.
  • Look like you slept.

5-second audio sting

"Dot. Dot. Dot. Done. Rufolia."

Why it works: the nickname and the sting repeat the same three-dot habit, so the product and the ritual stick together.

On claims: every line stays cosmetic ("brighter-looking", "look of dullness") and uses only facts from the public product pages: ingredients, prices, review counts and the 30-day guarantee. Final copy would go through your review team.

01 · Where I can help

What the team needs. What’s there now. What I’d do better.

Six things a D2C content strategist owns. For each one, I looked at what Honasa does today from public reports and its own campaigns, and wrote down what I’d change in my first months.

01Brand copy, content strategy and content cohorts
What’s there now

A house of brands where several sell the same thing. Mamaearth, The Derma Co and Aqualogica all sell sunscreen, so messages can blur.

What I’d do better

Give each brand one clear line, built on what the buyer needs: natural (Mamaearth), science (The Derma Co), light and fun (Aqualogica). Then plan content cohorts by customer stage: first order, second order, regular, lapsed.

Where I’ve done this: kept 4+ brand voices separate at Times Internet, and built content pillars and calendars at Signal.

02Campaign ideas, creative territories and brand stories
What’s there now

Strong campaign films and creator bursts, like Aqualogica’s Feels So Light, Shields So Right. Most ideas are launch-led and short-lived.

What I’d do better

Build territories that can run all year: Proof, not promises · Routine rituals · Desi skin truths in Hinglish humour. Plus one big D2C idea: “Your routine lives here.”

Where I’ve done this: recurring series at Signal, like Guess the Player and a three-part Ballon d’Or series, that people came back for.

03Turning consumer behaviour into content that converts
What’s there now

Excellent at the first purchase: ₹99 packs, quick commerce and a fast-growing offline presence. Repeat buying and loyalty are the weak links.

What I’d do better

Map triggers and barriers for each persona, then build content for the exact moments people drop off: Day 7 doubt, Day 25 refill, the abandoned cart.

Where I’ve done this: tracked 81 creator links down to installs and sign-ups to find which messages actually converted.

04Scripts and performance ads, short and long form
What’s there now

High creator volume. ASCI’s 2023 report named Honasa its top violator, with 187 ads, about 94% of them creator content.

What I’d do better

Hook-first scripts built on proof (wear tests, ingredient explainers, real results) and written to stay within ASCI rules from the first draft, not fixed later.

Where I’ve done this: reel scripts with a hook-to-CTA framework (3.87M Instagram views), and long-form content that grew TechGig to 300K+ monthly visits.

05RoAS on Meta and Google
What’s there now

Ad spend has been high, about 35% of sales in FY24. Honasa’s own plan looks for 100–150 bps of margin from more efficient channel spend.

What I’d do better

Spend smarter, not just more. Test 3 hooks per offer, cut the weakest fast, and retarget first-time buyers, who cost less than new ones. Use Google Search for buyers who already know what they want (“sunscreen for oily skin”). Judge RoAS and repeat rate together.

Where I’ve done this: daily reporting on installs and cost per install at Signal, used to decide what to make next.

06Working with designers, editors and other teams
What’s there now

Many brands, many teams and a lot of content going out fast.

What I’d do better

A one-page creative brief for every piece (sample below) and a weekly review where creative is judged against numbers, not opinions.

Where I’ve done this: briefed designers and video editors daily at Signal, and took agency clients from brief to final approval at OutSpark.

02 · Homework

How I read Honasa

I read the brand through five lenses: brand objective, growth equation, funnel, segmentation and competitive landscape.

Brand objective

₹2,392 Cr → ₹5,500 Cr

FY26 revenue to the FY31 target. Mamaearth to ₹2,000 Cr, The Derma Co to ₹1,500 Cr+, and two younger brands to ₹500 Cr each.

Momentum

Growth is back

Q2 FY27 update: early-30s% growth overall, with Mamaearth in the high teens and younger brands around the mid-40s%.

Where it wins

The first purchase

Big creator reach, ₹99 packs, quick commerce, and direct outlets planned to grow from about 1.2 lakh to 3 lakh+.

The opening

D2C is the smallest channel

In the FY31 plan, Honasa’s own site and app (₹400–450 Cr) is the smallest of its five channels. That’s room for the D2C team to grow.

The growth equation

Market×Penetration×Frequency×Amount per use=Brand size

Market

Being pushed: oral care, men’s grooming and fragrances.

Penetration

Being pushed: offline, tier-2 towns, ₹99 packs (people new to the category), and winning users from rivals (people new to the brand).

Frequency

Under-used. Repeat buying and full routines grow sales from people who already trust the brand.

Amount per use

Under-used. Teaching the right amount, like two fingers of sunscreen, helps both results and refills.

Funnel check

AwarenessStrong. Huge creator and digital reach.
ConsiderationTrust gap. Hype gets questioned.
TrialStrong. ₹99 packs, quick commerce.
RepeatWeak. Reorders go wherever is fastest.
LoyaltyWeak. “Flirty” users try new brands.
AdvocacyMixed. Backlash spreads fast.

Competitive landscape

Positioning

Natural vs proof

Mamaearth’s natural, toxin-free promise was new in 2016. Challengers like Minimalist, Foxtale and Plum now win on ingredient percentages and expert voices.

Every price point

From ₹99 packs to premium Luminéve. Price isn’t the gap. The gap is a reason to stay.

What they complain about: quality doubts, “too many ads”, claims that don’t match results.

03 · Ad research

What Honasa runs. What rivals run. What I’d build.

What Honasa’s ads look like today

Celebrity films

Famous faces, one per category

Shilpa Shetty for Mamaearth (“Hai Proof Tabhi Mama Approved”), Sreeleela for Mamaearth hair care, Sania Mirza for The Derma Co suncare (“1 sunscreen, 2 superpowers”).

Creators

Volume, now going regional

Creator-first since day one. A recent influencer mandate for south-specific agencies shows the push into regional markets.

Spend

₹744 Cr on marketing in FY25, and ₹206 Cr on ads in Q1 FY26 alone.

The pattern I see: almost every campaign I found is built to get noticed and win a first order. Very few speak to someone who has already bought.

Three gaps

Gap 1

Nobody owns the second order

Every brand fights for the first purchase. The reorder goes to whichever app is fastest.

Gap 2

Proof sits in the film, not the feed

A celebrity says it works. Rivals show it working on real people, with the active and the percentage.

Gap 3

The brand rarely explains itself

Challengers let founders and formulators talk. Honasa’s voice mostly comes from celebrities and creators.

04 · My idea

One problem. One idea.

The problem I’d solve first: a first-time buyer who never comes back to the brand’s own site.

The big idea

Your routine lives here.

Other apps sell you a product. The brand’s own site remembers your skin, your routine and when you’ll run out. That’s a reason to come back that no marketplace can copy.

Today they think“I’ll buy it wherever it’s fastest.”
→
We want them to think“The brand’s site knows my routine. I reorder there.”

“I want to convince first-time buyers that buying directly from the brand gets them a routine that works, not just a product.”

EssenceA one-off order becomes a relationship.
Solves the barrier“I’ll just buy wherever it’s fastest.”
MemorableFour words. Works for every brand and every channel.

Brand solution: the benefit ladder

Attributes

What we build

Skin quiz, routine bundles, refill reminders, loyalty points, early access, expert Q&A.

Functional

What it does

The right products for you, money saved, never running out.

Emotional

How it feels

Looked after. Confident in your skin, without thinking about it.

Reasons to believe

Why trust it

Verified reviews, clear ingredient information, expert-led brands, real customer results.

Media plan: paid, owned, earned

Paid

Meta + Google

Retarget first-time buyers with “complete your routine”. Search ads on high-intent terms. Creator partnership ads for the best organic reels.

Owned

Site, app, WhatsApp, Instagram

The skin quiz, how-to content, refill reminders, and saved routines with one-tap reorder.

Earned

Customers + creators

30-day result diaries, review carousels, and “gift a routine” referrals.

05 · Samples

Content cohorts, first order to fan

I’ve used Mamaearth as the example, since it’s the biggest brand and has its own site. The same system works for every brand.

First order

Find Your Routine

A 60-second skin quiz, promoted with reels and Story polls. Creators take it live and react to their result.

Days 1–7

Use It Right

Short how-to content so the first product actually works. “How much is enough?” “Morning or night?”

Days 7–21

Real Results

30-day diaries from real buyers, and reviews turned into carousels. Proof instead of hype.

Days 21–30

Running Low?

Refill reminders, “meet its best friend” pairings, and one-tap reorder on the brand’s site.

How I’d run it

Test, cut, scale

Run 3 hooks (humour, question, creator) against the same offer and budget. Cut the weakest once there’s enough data, scale the winner, and refresh creative every 2–3 weeks.

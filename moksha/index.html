How I worked

Three rules I write by

1. Find the fact firstEvery idea starts from something true on Moksha's own page or the brand's, like boAt's "boAtheads" fanbase. No invented claims.
2. Write many headlines, test the bestFor each project I wrote several lines, named the trigger each one pulls, and picked one to run and the others to test against it.
3. Make the reader actEvery piece ends with one clear action: shop, comment, tag or watch. If it can't earn a click, it isn't finished.

Anything marked [like this] is a fact or result only Moksha or the client has. I left it as a placeholder instead of making it up.

The campaign I'd pitch next · Wedding season

Shaadi season. Survival mode: on.

December means three weddings, forty relatives and one question at every table: "Beta, tumhari shaadi kab?" boAt earbuds become the polite escape. Smile, nod, and let the music talk.

  • Instagram postRishtedaar: "Beta, kab?" You, with earbuds in: 🎶🙂
  • Reel captionSangeet plan: dance for 10 minutes, then disappear into your playlist for 3 hours. Tag your shaadi survival partner 👇
  • 15-second reelAunty walks over. He slips in his earbuds and smiles. We hear only the beat. She gives up and pats his cheek. Text: "boAt. Shaadi season survival kit."

The creative · Instagram post, 1080 × 1350

Why it's built this way

  • The question is the hook. Every young Indian has heard it, so it stops the thumb in under a second.
  • The earbuds sit between the question and the answer. The product is the punchline, with no selling in words.
  • Marigold strings and bold red say "wedding" and "boAt" at the same time.
  • One action: tag a friend in the caption, shop in the button.

Headlines I tested on paper

HeadlineTriggerWhy
RunShaadi season. Survival mode: on.Relatable humourEvery young Indian has lived it. Short enough for a banner.
Test"Beta, shaadi kab?" Volume up.CuriosityOpens with the exact question, so people stop to see the answer.
TestFor every boAthead with 4 weddings this monthBelongingUses the fanbase name and makes members feel seen.
The ideaLead with the strongest fact: the fanbase has a name. That's the headline, not a generic "star is born".
The headlineThree lines, three triggers. Run the humour line, test the question and belonging lines against it.
The actionGive the joke a job. Every post ends with tag-a-friend or shop-the-kit, so the laugh turns into reach or a sale.

The trade-off I weighed: A hard offer, like a wedding-season deal with a deadline, could lift sales, but it risks boAt's cool image. My call: keep the hero line pure, and put any offer in the second line and the stories.

Rewritten case study headline

One voice for Mamaearth, from the guidelines to the shampoo bottle

Brief
Give Mamaearth one clear voice and help shoppers use its products right.

Insight
Online shoppers can't ask a store assistant. A 20-second tutorial is the assistant.

Idea
Guidelines people can actually use, and tutorials that teach one thing in under 20 seconds.

Work
Brand guidelines covering identity, voice and creative, plus tutorial videos for product pages.

Results
[tutorial views] · [product page conversion]

The creative · tutorial cover for Reels and Stories, 1080 × 1920

Why it's built this way

  • The hook is the whole headline. It hints at a mistake, so people tap to find out.
  • Three steps, readable without sound, because most people watch Stories on mute.
  • Mamaearth's calm greens keep it gentle and family-safe.
  • "Watch how" and the product name close the cover, so the shopper knows what to do and what to buy.

Voice in practice · a one-page cheat sheet

We sayWe don't sayWhy
Made with onion and plant keratin.Infused with the power of nature's miracle.Name the ingredient. Skip the hype.
Massage into your scalp for 2 minutes.Indulge in a luxurious ritual.Tell people exactly what to do.
Leaves hair feeling soft and clean.The best shampoo you'll ever try.A claim we can prove beats a superlative.

20-second tutorial script · Onion Shampoo

TimeOn screenVoiceover / text
0–3sClose-up of a hand rubbing shampoo only on the hair lengths."Washing your hair? Wash your scalp."
3–10sCoin-sized amount on palm, applied at the roots."A coin-sized amount, straight onto wet roots."
10–16sFingertips massaging in small circles."Massage for two minutes, then rinse."
16–20sBottle with the product name."Mamaearth Onion Shampoo. Use it right, every time."

Steps to be matched to the instructions on the pack before shooting.

The ideaGuidelines are only useful with examples. A "we say / we don't say" table teaches the voice in ten seconds.
The headline"Washing your hair? Wash your scalp." works because it suggests a mistake the viewer might be making, which makes them watch.
The actionA tutorial on a product page is selling time. End on the product name and one clear instruction, nothing else.

The trade-off I weighed: A fear hook like "3 mistakes ruining your hair wash" might stop more people, but it doesn't suit a gentle, family-safe brand. My call: test the softer "wash your scalp" hook first.

The creative · website and marketplace banner, 1920 × 640

Why it's built this way

  • The brand name is in the headline, so even a two-second glance tells you who's talking.
  • A clock at six, a sunrise and a steaming mug tell the 6 AM story before anyone reads a word.
  • The subline answers "how easy?" in one line, then the button asks for the sale.
  • Works as a carousel set: swap the subline for each member of the 6 AM club.

A+ content · five blocks

  • Block 1 · HeroBru. For the 6 AM club. The first quiet cup before the house wakes up.
  • Block 2 · The tasteRich, strong and familiar. [blend details from the pack]
  • Block 3 · How to make itOne spoon, hot water or milk, a quick stir. Your cup is ready before the kettle cools.
  • Block 4 · Who it's forThe mum packing three tiffins. The dad reading the paper twice. You, before your first meeting.
  • Block 5 · Pick your pack[pack sizes and prices]. Stock up for winter mornings.
The ideaOne big idea across every touchpoint. Facts like the blend go in as placeholders until checked against the pack.
The headlineBanners live or die by click-through. Three triggers, one winner to run, two to test.
The actionThe last A+ block must sell: pack sizes, price, and a reason to buy now, like stocking up for winter.

The trade-off I weighed: Putting the price in the first banner could drive quick clicks, but it would lose Bru's warmth. My call: emotion in the hero banner, price in the retargeting banner.

My version

Rewritten case study headline

How Kimirica made luxury feel like a pause

Brief
Make Kimirica feel as premium online as it does in your hands.

Insight
Luxury buyers don't want more products. They want a moment for themselves.

Idea
Gift someone a pause. Every piece of copy sells the moment first, then the product.

Work
Website banners, A+ content, social posts and a content strategy.

Results
[reach] · [engagement rate] · [gifting sales]

Content pillars

  • The pauseSlow rituals: baths, evenings, Sunday mornings.
  • The sensesScent, texture and ingredients, told up close.
  • The giftWho to gift, when, and what to write on the card.

The creative · Instagram post, 1080 × 1350

Why it's built this way

  • Lots of empty space is the luxury signal. Nothing shouts.
  • A soft serif headline reads like a handwritten gift card.
  • The line underneath names a real person ("the friend who replies to emails at midnight"), so people tag that friend.
  • Sage and sand tones feel calm and premium on a busy feed.

Social and banner copy

  • Homepage bannerThis season, gift someone a pause. [Shop gift sets]
  • Instagram postFor the friend who replies to emails at midnight: twenty quiet minutes, wrapped.
  • Gift guideFor her: a slow evening. For him: a long shower he didn't rush. For you: the same. You've earned it.
  • Gift card lineOpen this when the world gets loud.
The ideaLuxury is built by repetition. "A pause" should appear on the banner, the box and every post, so it becomes the brand's word.
The headline"For the friend who replies to emails at midnight" names a real person, so readers tag that friend. Specific beats general.
The actionGifting has a deadline. Add a real reason to buy now, like the last date for delivery before the holidays.

The trade-off I weighed: Urgency sells gifts, but shouting cheapens a luxury brand. My call: calm hero copy, and one quiet line about the delivery cut-off near the button.
